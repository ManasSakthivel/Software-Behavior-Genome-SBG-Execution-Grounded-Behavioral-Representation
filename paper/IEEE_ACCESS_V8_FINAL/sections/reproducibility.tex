\section{Reproducibility and Artifact Audit}\label{sec:repro}

\subsection{Replication Package}
The implementation, development records, raw result files and the manuscript sources are in the project repository on branch \code{research/v8-linux-validation}. The manuscript directory contains the scripts that regenerate every number and figure in this paper from machine-readable records: \code{verify\_five\_dataset.py} for the artifact audit below, \code{stats\_recompute.py} for all statistics (out-of-fold predictions are regenerated with the repository's cross-validation code and checked against the saved log loss and Brier scores, which they reproduce exactly), \code{make\_stat\_figures.py} and \code{make\_diagrams.py} for the figures, and \code{build\_security\_outcomes.py} and \code{audit\_security\_rerun.py} for the security tables. A reproducing group needs Python~3.11 or later with NumPy, SciPy and Matplotlib; the statistical recomputation takes about one minute and does not execute any program under test. Re-collecting the evidence additionally requires the V7-B engine and an operating-system sandbox (\code{sandbox-exec} on macOS, \code{bwrap} on Linux). The \sbg{} test suite (\code{tests/v8}) passed with 247 tests, 8 skipped and 2 expected failures---the two profile-defect tests that the fix resolves---on macOS at the final commit of the evaluated branch. A claim--evidence register (\code{NUMERICAL\_CLAIM\_AUDIT.md}) maps every numerical statement in this paper to its source file. The placebo, frozen-threshold and profile-fix results come from branch \code{research/v8-mac-hardening} (commits \code{8e28f601}, \code{b9b8a86c}).

\subsection{Artifact Audit}
Before writing this paper we audited the project's machine-readable records against its internal reports. Three findings affect what can be claimed, and we report them because they bear on the reproducibility of research prototypes generally.

\paragraph*{An evaluation artifact that did not meet the evidential standard} The repository contains a ``five-dataset'' execution artifact of 250 pairs labelled with the names of Defects4J, BugsInPy, Bears, BugSwarm and Bugs.jar, together with reports describing it as an independent execution in containers. The audit found that the pairs are generated from twelve hand-written code templates with synthetic commit identifiers, that no program was executed, and that each input's outcome was assigned from the pair's label. The verdicts therefore reproduce the labels by construction, and the artifact carries no evidential weight about detection, false-assurance risk or reproducibility. We exclude it entirely; at most it shows that the record schema, profile, \ers{} harness and prediction lock run end to end on 250 records. A genuine external evaluation remains future work (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph*{Report figures without raw support} Some figures in internal reports could not be traced to any raw output, including calibration slopes near 1, a strict-policy coverage of 47 pairs, and per-seed $p$-values; others reported container isolation through a runtime that the code never invokes. None of these is used. Every number in this paper was recomputed from raw records.

\paragraph*{A profile defect fixed on a different branch} The evaluated branch predates the profile fix described in Section~\ref{sec:approach}, which exists on a sibling branch. We report the evaluated branch's numbers and the effect of the fix separately; integrating the fix into the evaluated branch is required before any further study.

\subsection{Adjudication Protocol and Prediction Lock}
The adjudication protocol, implemented and tested on synthetic fixtures but not yet executed, has two adjudicators examine each change independently within a boundary $\obs$ and budget $\budget$ that are frozen before the first decision. The case schema has no field for model scores, \ers{} outputs or verdicts, and any such key raises an error. A disagreement goes to a third, distinct adjudicator whose decision is final; without one, the case stays unresolved and enters the estimand through the variants of Section~\ref{sec:problem}. Agreement between the two primary adjudicators is reported as Cohen's $\kappa$ with the confusion matrix, and scoring may begin only if $\kappa\ge0.7$ on at least 30\% double-labelled cases. Decisions are recorded in an append-only hash-chained log, which is tamper-evident but not signed. Predictions are hash-sealed before adjudication. The audit showed that a usable lock requires an external timestamp and an adjudication package from which labels cannot be inferred from identifiers, descriptions or patch comments; both are prerequisites of the confirmatory study.
