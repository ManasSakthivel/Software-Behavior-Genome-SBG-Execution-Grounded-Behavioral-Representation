# Research Gap and Novelty Assessment (adversarial; pass 2)

Stance: a reviewer who knows the testing literature looks for prior art for each claim. Scale: **Novel** (no prior art found), **Partially novel** (components exist; the specific combination or feature not found), **Not novel** (prior art found), **Unsupported** (cannot be assessed from this evidence). "Found none" means within this limited review (single reviewer; 8 own-read and 12 tool-mediated full texts, 89 abstracts, 76 metadata-only records; ACM DL inaccessible) and is not an absence proof. No "first" claim is warranted for any item. Published findings and SBG's own findings are kept apart.

## C1. Witness representation: Partially novel (narrowed)
Prior art: verification witnesses are exchangeable, independently validated artefacts (Beyer et al. TOSEM 2022; FSE 2015/2016; abstracts). In cross-version testing, executable witnesses are routine: Shadow replays divergent inputs natively on both versions and also reports code-level divergences (full text); Mokav's DET is a unit test asserting different outputs (tool-mediated full text); Testora reports the test, both outputs and an explanation (tool-mediated); PatchGuru produces a comparison program with runtime assertions (tool-mediated); Evans & Savoia's regressed/progressed test sets are executable (full text). Attack: "a witness is a failing input plus two outputs". Surviving claim: one schema that also carries observation context (what was exercised, environment, seeds), is used for no-difference outcomes too, and is checkable by a third party. No source read defines that; this is an absence claim.

## C2. Explicit sufficiency: Partially novel, now only as a gate (weakest)
New prior art in pass 2: Evans & Savoia 2007 (full text) state that an empty difference set is ambiguous because the generated suites may be inadequate; DiffTestGen defines a union coverage metric over changed lines in old and new versions and reports it per dataset (tool-mediated); Cleverest measures "commit reached" with GCOV and ranks outcomes bug triggered > output changed > commit reached > none (tool-mediated); Testora measures diff coverage (RQ2); SemaDiff measures coverage of changed code and dependent classes; patch coverage studies (Hilton 2018, Bailey & Cadar 2025; abstracts). Earlier: dynamic impact analysis authors say results hold for observed executions (Law & Rothermel; Orso, full text); Daikon's authors say accuracy depends on test quality (full text).
Attack: "sufficiency is change coverage, which tools already compute and use". That attack succeeds for the measure. Surviving claim: a recorded, per-claim field that conditions the verdict. In the texts read, coverage is used for generation feedback or evaluation, not as a verdict condition (inference).
Additional risk: coverage is itself a contested proxy: Shadow's abstract (covering a patch often exercises the same behaviour in old and new code), Inozemtseva & Holmes (title), Just et al. 2014 (73% coupled, 17% not), and Voas PIE and checked coverage (snippet-level). SBG's own red-team found that its S2 level can be reached from two inputs and showed no measurable discrimination on synthetic labels (internal finding).

## C3. Evidence-grounded insufficient outcome: Partially novel (narrow); the abstain idea is Not novel
Prior art: DiffKemp's equal / non-equal / undetermined (full text), with non-equal meaning "not proven equal"; Prefactory's pass / fail / inconclusive with "evidence, not proof" (tool-mediated; inconclusive = script cannot run reliably); ORION's human-review route for infrastructure failures (tool-mediated); Cleverest's ordinal "reached but unchanged"; Evans & Savoia's ambiguity statement; regression verification's sound direction; reject option (Chow 1970; Geifman 2017; Hendrickx 2024; Jeong 2025 for code), grounded on model confidence. Contrast cases: Testora, DiffTestGen, SemaDiff, Mokav give binary or "not detected" outputs; PatchGuru's empty report conflates cases (text as processed).
Attack: "this is just 'unknown' with coverage". Surviving claim: the grounds are recorded observation evidence about the change, and the outcome never reads as equivalence (offline equivalence is undecidable; Godlin & Strichman abstract; Tian et al.). Prefactory shows an inconclusive class for execution-based version checks is not defensible as new by itself.

## C4. Reproducibility-aware comparison: mechanism Not novel; integration Partially novel
Prior art: Testora re-executes differences and its classifier asks whether the difference is deterministic; DiffTestGen discards tests whose results change on re-run; Cleverest repeats runs; Shadow's authors state replay non-determinism as a limit; per-test flakiness literature (iDFlakies, DeFlaker, Luo, Parry, FlakyLens) is extensive. Earlier assessment text that this was unverifiable for Testora/DiffTestGen is replaced by the above. Attack: "re-run N times is not a contribution". Surviving claim: typing nondeterminism into the verdict and witness for cross-version comparison. SBG internal evidence: over 311 pairs the replay and control-alternation machinery differed from a plain repeat-three-times baseline on 2 pairs (red-team review), so no benefit is demonstrated.

## C5. Empirical evidence: Unsupported by this review
The review can only record the standards prior work met: Evans & Savoia (one project, 38 seeded faults); Shadow (18 patches); Mokav (1,535 competition pairs); Testora (525 checked PRs, 164 labelled classifier entries); DiffTestGen (463 PRs); PatchGuru (400 PRs); ORION (154-PR balanced benchmark with Testora, PatchGuru and LLM-agent baselines); SemaDiff (183 labelled Java commits); Cleverest (72 commits with a directed-fuzzer baseline). Most are Python PR benchmarks from the same four projects (keras, marshmallow, pandas, scipy), so independence between those studies is limited (inference). Evidence for SBG must come from SBG's own independent runs. Baselines a reviewer will demand: Shadow/KLEE-style (for C), Testora, DiffTestGen, PatchGuru, ORION and Mokav-style LLM differential testing, DiffKemp, Daikon diffing, plain change-coverage, and a repeat-k differential baseline. Pitfall controls from P0 (de-duplication, label-noise audit, time/project split, size-only and JIT baselines, abstention risk-coverage) remain necessary (Allamanis 2019; Herzig 2013; Jimenez 2019; Kapoor & Narayanan 2023).

## Overall
The defensible contribution is integration: one replayable contract with witnesses for difference, a recorded sufficiency field that gates a no-divergence verdict, an insufficient outcome with recorded grounds, and typed nondeterminism, under controls that were designed before results. Every primitive has prior art, and the 2026 differential-testing cluster (Testora, DiffTestGen, PatchGuru, ORION, Prefactory, SemaDiff) already contains parts of C2-C4. Reviewers can truthfully say: executable witnesses exist; change coverage is measured and used; three-way outcomes exist; re-execution against flakiness exists. Claims should be phrased as "we found none" for the gate and the grounds, avoid "first" and "novel" for any primitive, and make no performance claim relative to any named tool. Published results about those tools are the authors' own and were not reproduced here.

Limitations: 12 full-text reads are tool-mediated; many prior-art statements rest on abstracts or titles; two claims (C1 context schema, C2 gate) turn on absence in unread or unavailable texts (Shadow TOSEM, Rutledge & Orso, Jin et al. were not read).
