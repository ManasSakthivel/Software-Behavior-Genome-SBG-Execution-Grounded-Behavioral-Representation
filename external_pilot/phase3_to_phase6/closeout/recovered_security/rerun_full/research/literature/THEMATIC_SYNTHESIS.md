# Thematic Synthesis

Basis: `EVIDENCE_MATRIX.csv` (185 rows after pass 2: 20 FULL TEXT, of which 8 own read and 12 read through a summarising fetch of arXiv HTML; 89 ABSTRACT; 76 METADATA). Statements carry the access level of the cited source; "inference" marks reviewer reasoning. Cite keys are in `VERIFIED_REFERENCES.bib`. Published findings are separated from SBG's own findings (the latter only in the last section). This is a targeted review, not systematic, not PRISMA, single reviewer.

## T1. Comparing versions by execution is old; the field now generates the comparing tests with LLMs
Cross-version differential testing was formulated by Evans & Savoia (ESEC/FSE 2007; full text): generate characterisation tests for both versions and contrast them; 21%, 34%, 21% more behaviour changes than regression testing on three versions of one project (JTopas, 38 seeded faults). Other precedents: McKeeman 1998 (secondary), Jin, Orso, Xie 2010 (metadata), DiffGen 2008 (metadata), Rutledge & Orso ICST 2022 (metadata), Godefroid et al. ISSTA 2020 (abstract, REST API versions), and Shadow symbolic execution (ICSE 2016, full text; 18 CoREBench patches). 2024-26 LLM work, all arXiv or recent venues, mostly Python PR benchmarks: Mokav (JSS 2025; 1,535 Codeforces pairs + 32 QuixBugs; full text, tool-mediated), Testora (ICSE 2026; 525 checked PRs, 19 regressions), Cleverest (PACMSE FSE 2026; 72 commits in 8 C/C++/JS/Python programs), DiffTestGen (arXiv Jul 2026; 463 PRs), PatchGuru (arXiv 2026; 400 PRs, precision 0.62 vs Testora 0.32 per its authors), ORION (arXiv Oct 2026; 154-PR labelled benchmark), DiffSpec (spec-driven, implementations), Prefactory and SemaDiff (refactoring/semantic-change checks). Theme: finding a difference is well served and several tools now report real developer-confirmed regressions. Reporting what is known when no difference is found is addressed in the texts read only by silence or by binary outputs (Testora, DiffTestGen, PatchGuru, SemaDiff, Mokav), with exceptions noted in T3.

## T2. "Did the tests touch the change?" is measured, and used as generation feedback, not as a verdict condition
Patch/change coverage is studied empirically (Hilton et al. ASE 2018, abstract: 7,816 builds of 47 projects, statement coverage of patches; Bailey & Cadar ICST 2025, abstract: nine C/C++ projects over 78 years; Covrig 2014, metadata). Cross-version testers use it operationally: DiffTestGen's union coverage (covered changed lines in old plus new over all changed lines; 92.7% on Testora's PRs), Cleverest's "commit reached" via GCOV, Testora's diff coverage (52-81% of generated tests execute at least one changed line), SemaDiff's coverage of changed code, KATCH and test-suite augmentation (metadata). One published caution applies to coverage as sufficiency: Shadow's abstract (tests that cover a patch often exercise the same behaviour in old and new code). Theme: change coverage is established; making it a recorded condition of the verdict is not seen in the texts read (inference; absence claim from a limited search).

## T3. Three-way and inconclusive outcomes exist in static tools and in some LLM-era differential checks
Static tools: DiffKemp (full text) reports equal / non-equal / could not be determined, the last mostly for missing definitions; its non-equal is "not proven equal". Regression verification and SymDiff target proofs. Execution-based: Prefactory (arXiv 2026) defines differential-test outcomes pass / fail / inconclusive (script cannot run reliably) and states that a pass is evidence on generated inputs, not proof; ORION routes infrastructure-failure-only PRs to human review; Cleverest's ordinal result separates "commit reached" from "none"; Evans & Savoia (2007) state that an empty difference set is ambiguous (identical versions or inadequate tests). PatchGuru's empty report does not separate "consistent" from "budget exhausted" (per the text read). Theme: an inconclusive class is not novel; what those classes are grounded on (analysability, harness failure, generation budget) differs from "the change was not adequately exercised".

## T4. Witnesses are mature in verification; in cross-version testing they are executable tests
Verification witnesses (Beyer et al. TOSEM 2022; FSE 2015/2016; abstracts) are exchangeable and independently validated. In cross-version testing the witness is a concrete input or test: Shadow's divergent inputs replayed natively, Mokav's DET unit test, Testora's test with both outputs and an explanation, PatchGuru's comparison program with assertions. No source read defines a common replayable format covering both difference and no-difference outcomes together with observation context (absence claim, limited search).

## T5. Flakiness and nondeterminism: per-test literature, plus ad hoc handling inside differential tools
Per-test literature: Luo et al. 2014 (metadata), iDFlakies, DeFlaker (metadata), Parry et al. 2021 survey, FlakyLens 2025 (snippet-level), industrial studies. Inside cross-version tools: Testora re-executes differences and asks a classifier whether the difference is deterministic; DiffTestGen discards tests whose results change on re-run; Cleverest repeats runs and reports no observed flakiness; Shadow's authors acknowledge non-determinism in replay and in their automatic classification; PatchGuru, SemaDiff and Prefactory describe no handling in the text read; ORION isolates environments and measures LLM variance without reruns. Mokav measures LLM sampling variance, not program nondeterminism. Theme: repetition to filter nondeterminism is standard; an earlier statement in this review that Testora's nondeterminism handling was "not evaluated" is corrected.

## T6. Adequacy research warns that coverage is a weak proxy for evidence strength
Zhu et al. 1997; Inozemtseva & Holmes 2014 (title: coverage not strongly correlated with effectiveness; metadata); Kochhar 2015; Papadakis 2018; Just et al. FSE 2014 (357 faults; 73% coupled, 17% not coupled; abstract-level); Schuler & Zeller 2011/2013 (checked coverage: covered code that influences an oracle; snippet-level); Voas 1992 (execution, infection, propagation; snippet-level); Masri & Abou Assi (coincidental correctness; metadata). Inference: reaching changed code does not establish that a difference would have been observable; a sufficiency level based on line/branch coverage of the changed region is a contested proxy.

## T7. Equivalence, equivalent mutants and undecidability
Offline equivalence is undecidable in general (Godlin & Strichman abstract; Tian et al. ISSTA 2024, full text tool-mediated, state it for mutants and report F1 of 86.58% for the best fine-tuned model against 50.8% for trivial compiler equivalence, on 3,302 Java pairs). Trivial Compiler Equivalence (Papadakis 2015) and constraint-based detection (Offutt & Pan 1997) are sound-direction heuristics (abstract level). EquiBench (2025, abstract) evaluates LLM judgement of equivalence. Theme: equivalence is judged by proof, heuristics or models; none is an observation-grounded "no divergence observed" statement. A tool that emits only non-equivalence evidence and never "equivalent" is consistent with this literature.

## T8. Abstention is developed for model uncertainty
Chow 1970; Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017; SelectiveNet; Hendrickx 2024; Kamath 2020; Wen et al. 2025 (survey of abstention in LLMs, metadata); in code: Spiess et al. ICSE 2025, Jeong et al. (selective code generation with test-based checks; arXiv, ICML 2026 per comment), Sharma & David 2025 and Rathnasuriya & Yang (ICSE 2026 NIER) at snippet level. All abstain on model confidence or on the correctness of generated code. None read abstains because the version change was not observed adequately (inference from abstracts). Risk-coverage reporting is the established evaluation vocabulary.

## T9. Execution traces as representations, and behaviour-as-oracle risks
TRACED, CodeExecutor, NExT (ICML 2024 per search result; abstract-level) use execution traces as model inputs; Whole Program Paths (Larus 1999) and dynamic impact analysis use traces as analysis data (full text for Law & Rothermel and Orso 2004). Charoosaei et al. 2026 find that 8.4-16.9% of LLM-generated regression tests are fault-enforcing (they encode faulty behaviour) and that 83-91% of propagated fault-enforcing tests still accepted their fault (full text, tool-mediated). Inference: agreement between versions is not correctness, which is why a no-difference output must not be worded as assurance.

## T10. Evaluation reproducibility and leakage
Gonzalez-Barahona & Robles 2012/2023, Shepperd 2014/2018, Tantithamthavorn 2017, Timperley 2021, Collberg & Proebsting 2016, Kapoor & Narayanan 2023, Gundersen & Kjensmo 2018, Allamanis 2019 (duplicates can inflate reported performance by up to 100%), Herzig 2013 (label noise), Arcuri & Briand 2012, Klees et al. 2018. 2026 differential-testing papers state benchmark limits themselves (four Python projects; possible training-data exposure; single-researcher labels; abstract and body numbers that do not reconcile in DiffTestGen and SemaDiff as processed). These motivate, but do not substitute for, SBG's own controls.

## T11. Methodology for this review
Kitchenham et al. 2004, Dyba et al. 2005, Runeson & Host 2009, Petersen 2008, Cruzes & Dyba 2011 inform the approach loosely (single reviewer, no dual screening, no pre-registration).

## Cross-cutting gaps in the evidence base
1. Observability and trace-capture systems are thinly covered (2 rows).
2. 76 of 185 rows are metadata only; their relevance statements are reviewer inference.
3. 12 of 20 full-text reads are tool-mediated; "not described" statements for them are weaker than own-read ones. ACM DL was inaccessible; Shadow TOSEM 2018 stays at abstract level.
4. No new tool was run; no claim about relative performance is made or supported.
