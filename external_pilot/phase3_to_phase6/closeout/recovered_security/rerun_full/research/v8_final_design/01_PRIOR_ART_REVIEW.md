## Papers read in full text in this sprint
1. **Schuler & Zeller, Assessing Oracle Quality with Checked Coverage (ICST 2011, DOI 10.1109/icst.2011.32).** Question: do coverage metrics assess whether results are actually checked? Method: checked coverage = the dynamic slice of covered statements that influence an oracle; 7 open-source Java projects; compared with statement coverage and mutation score. Finding: checked coverage is more sensitive than the alternatives to degraded oracles. **Evidence-deletion experiment:** they progressively remove assertions (25-75%, and all) and show checked coverage decays faster than statement coverage or mutation score (their Fig. 6; with all checks removed checked coverage is 0% by construction). Limits: Java, oracle quality only, no version pair, no probability, no calibration. Relation to CEC: (a) the *deletion intervention* and its use as a sensitivity test of an adequacy measure are established prior art; (b) *influence on an oracle* is the closest relative of our D2. CEC would have to differ by operating on a differential observation boundary and by feeding responses to a calibrated risk model. The 2013 STVR extension (10.1002/stvr.1497) was not read.
2. **Fraser & Walkinshaw, Assessing and generating test sets in terms of behavioural adequacy (STVR 2015, DOI 10.1002/stvr.1575).** Adequacy is the predictive accuracy of a model inferred from test executions, estimated by k-fold cross-validation over tests; used for search-based test generation; Java units; higher behavioural coverage detects more faults. Limits stated in the paper include limits of model inference and scalability. Relation: leave-out evidence evaluation is established; their target is fault detection, ours is the reliability of a *no-divergence* verdict on a version pair. Read in part (summary, introduction, CV section, headings), not end to end.
3. **Paul & Holmes, Beyond Coverage and Kill Scores: Empirically Measuring Test Suite Behavioural Gaps (arXiv 2606.10417, preprint, 9 Jun 2026).** Defines a behavioural gap as a documented behaviour not validated by any existing test; LLM-based extraction (Qwen3-14B) and mapping; 10 Java libraries from Defects4J, 8,922 documented methods, 20,729 behaviours (extraction precision 93.1%), estimated gap 17.5%, EvoSuite and ASTER gaps 20.6% and 27.1%; gap weakly related to line coverage (rho 0.112) and kill score (rho 0.377, R2 0.162); gaps persist in 38.2% of methods with 100% line coverage. Limits: conservative lower bound, recall not measured, LLM judge, Java/JUnit only, public/protected documented methods. No tests are modified or ablated; no counterfactuals, no calibration, no differential/regression comparison between versions (verified by reading, and by keyword checks over the extracted text: 0 hits for ablation, counterfactual, calibrat, differential). **Verdict: not a competing contribution to false-assurance calibration; it is the strongest 2026 support for the premise that structural coverage does not capture behavioural adequacy.** Reviewer risk: they will ask why documented-behaviour coverage is not a feature; it is listed as a non-goal (needs documentation and LLM judge; recall unknown).
4. **Angelopoulos, Bates, Fisch, Lei, Schuster, Conformal Risk Control (ICLR 2024; arXiv 2208.02814 v4).** Controls E[L_{n+1}(lambda_hat)] <= alpha for exchangeable, non-increasing, bounded (by B) loss functions; lambda_hat = inf{lambda : n/(n+1) * R_hat_n(lambda) + B/(n+1) <= alpha}; tight up to O(1/n) under continuity; non-monotone risk is not controlled. Consequences for CEC: the guarantee is in expectation over the calibration draw (not conditional on the test projects, not high-probability), needs exchangeable units (projects, not pairs), and is a bound on an expected *joint* loss. The conditional risk P(Y=1 | A=1) is a ratio and is **not** directly controlled. Hence formal guarantees are secondary and conditional on assumption A-EX (04).

## Items accessed at abstract/metadata level only
Geifman & El-Yaniv selective classification (arXiv 1705.08500, preprint; abstract: user-set risk, guarantee "with high probability"); Selective Conformal Risk Control (Xu, Guo, Wei, arXiv 2512.12844, preprint: select then control); Bai & Jin, Conformal Selective Prediction with General Risk Control (arXiv 2603.24704, preprint; the brief's title differs): e-value based accept/abstain under bounded risk; conformal for hierarchical/clustered data (Dunn et al., cluster trials) only through search snippets; conservative Bayesian inference for failure-free operation (Zhao, Strigini et al.; snippets only). These establish that selective risk control exists and that clustering and selection complicate exchangeability; their exact assumptions must be read before any guarantee is claimed.
