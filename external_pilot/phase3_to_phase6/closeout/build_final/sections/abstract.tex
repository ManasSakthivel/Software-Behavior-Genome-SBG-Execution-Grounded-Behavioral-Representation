Differential regression testing acts on disagreement, but an empty difference set is read as reassurance, although it reports only that its inputs showed nothing on the compared channels. We reformulate such a no-divergence verdict as a selective prediction about its evidence and define its false-assurance risk relative to a declared observation boundary and adjudication budget, distinct from equivalence. Three results follow. A risk measured under one boundary is a lower bound for any wider one. Any verdict issued on unvalidated evidence can be forced by an adversary, so integrity must gate every verdict. Conformance of a risk estimator to evidence-response properties is logically independent of its accuracy. SBG~V8 operationalises the formulation with a change-conditioned evidence profile, an integrity-first abstaining decision procedure, and an Evidence Response Signature of eight label-free properties tested by evidence perturbations. In a pilot on 312 executed development pairs from synthetic programs, QuixBugs and BugsInPy (258 with construction labels), plain differential testing had an observed false-assurance risk of 0.169. The predecessor's sufficiency gate withheld two thirds of those verdicts without lowering the risk, no better than random abstention. The signature exposed a profile defect invisible to aggregate metrics, observed in an exploratory real-execution matrix whose run directories are absent, and showed that the predecessor ignored tampered evidence. The richer evidence was not shown to improve prediction: at matched coverage it differed from coverage-only calibration by 1.6 percentage points (95\% interval $-6.6$ to $+16.3$), and only effects near 16 points were detectable. The data are single-language and unadjudicated. A single run on a separate v8 test split (956 pairs) gave the composite score labelled sbg\_v5 an AUROC of 0.694, below reversed edit size (0.876); the pre-declared verdict was NOT DEMONSTRATED. No independent, confirmatory held-out benchmark evaluation was successfully established.
