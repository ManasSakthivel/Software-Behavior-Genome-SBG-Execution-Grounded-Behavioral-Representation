# WPD-D4 — Independent statistical assessment of the external BugsInPy / QuixBugs results

*Agent: WP-D subagent D4. Scope: statistics only. This document does not make the IEEE Access submission decision, does not
re-score V10/V9c, and does not tune anything on external labels. All tables below are generated by
`scripts/d4_08_render.py` directly from the JSON/CSV outputs of `scripts/d4_01…d4_07`; narrative numbers are asserted against
those outputs by `scripts/d4_09_check_report.py`.*

**Label legend (used in every table).**
`PRE-REG` = an analysis defined in `SBG/docs/current/PHASE2_PREREGISTRATION.md` §5/§6 (ALL / EVALUABLE(=VALID) denominators,
cluster bootstrap by program/bug, within-cluster permutation, committed v7 scores, **no SP-2 exclusion**, canonical input regime
primary and declared regime secondary for QuixBugs).
`POST-HOC` = anything else, including the two headline numbers under review (0.497 and 0.444). Post-hoc ≠ wrong; it only means the
reported p-values and CIs carry no pre-specified error rate.

## 0. Bottom line (statistics only)

1. **0.497 (BugsInPy) and 0.444 (QuixBugs) are reproduced exactly (0.497044, 0.444444) from the raw per-pair scores, but both are
   POST-HOC numbers.** Each requires (a) the v8 corrected-harness rescore and (b) deleting *every* SP-2 negative (BugsInPy 147→107
   pairs, QuixBugs 151→111). Neither the rescore nor the exclusion is in the pre-registration. The **pre-registered** values are
   BugsInPy EVALUABLE/VALID **0.4099**, BugsInPy ALL **0.4340**, QuixBugs canonical ALL **0.3877**.
2. **The pre-registered results are *below* chance, not at chance.** BugsInPy-EVAL 0.410 (bug-cluster 95 % CI 0.329–0.489, permutation
   p(AUROC<0.5)=0.015; project-cluster CI 0.354–0.529, p=0.031) and QuixBugs-canonical 0.388 (CI 0.290–0.495, p=0.007). The data
   therefore do **not** support "SBG is at chance"; they support "SBG ranks real-bug pairs *lower* than SP-transform pairs on average".
   (Directional tests were not pre-specified; both tails are reported in §3.)
3. **The post-hoc ex-SP-2 numbers are statistically uninformative about a direction.** 0.497 [0.401, 0.593] and 0.444 [0.356, 0.536]
   both contain 0.5 and both are consistent with true AUROC anywhere from ≈0.40 to ≈0.59. They rule out any AUROC ≥ 0.6 (narrowly for the BugsInPy
   project-cluster CI), ≥ 0.75, and V10-level 0.897, but they do **not** establish "AUROC ≤ 0.5". Deleting SP-2 negatives is a selection that
   removes exactly the negatives on which SBG scores worst (SP-2-only AUROC 0.28–0.31); the "corrected" number is therefore not an unbiased
   estimate of the pre-registered quantity, and which negatives are removed is a researcher degree of freedom (§7, §8).
4. **"SBG does not generalise" vs "inconclusive":** the data support *"no evidence of generalisation (≥ 0.6) on these two corpora under
   the output-free protocol"*. They do **not** support a precise "at chance" claim, and they do not demonstrate a mechanism. The
   repository's mechanism claim ("63–82 % of positives score 0, therefore AUROC cannot exceed chance in expectation") is **not valid as stated** (§4).
5. **Label–source confound.** In every external corpus positives are developer edits and negatives are SBG transformer output. Trivial
   provenance features separate the labels perfectly (`variant_is_unparse_canonical`: AUROC 0.000 = a perfect 1.000 when reversed, in BugsInPy and both
   QuixBugs corpora) or strongly (reversed `edit_lines` ≈ 0.92 BugsInPy). Hence the external AUROC measures *"developer edit vs. SBG transformer"* as much as *"behaviour change vs. none"*; this
   weakens the external task as a validation of the V10 task (§5). Reasoning about direction is possible, but not about SBG's behavioural validity.
6. **QuixBugs declared-input regime is above chance** (pre-registered secondary regime, EVALUABLE 0.702 [0.580, 0.825], permutation
   p(AUROC>0.5)=0.0003) — reported plainly. Caveats: only 28 of 40 programs are evaluable (survivorship), declared inputs come from the QuixBugs test
   files and may be bug-revealing, the result was first published only in an exploratory diagnostic, and **a reversed `static_token` baseline (1 − 0.182 = 0.818) reaches a higher AUROC than SBG on the same pairs** (§5).
7. **Power:** with 39 bug clusters / 11 projects (BugsInPy) and 40 programs (QuixBugs) the design can only detect (80 % power, one-sided α = 2.5 %)
   a true AUROC of ≈ 0.61–0.68 (approximation, §6). A true AUROC of 0.55–0.60 is not distinguishable from 0.5 here.
8. **Forking paths:** 21 distinct external AUROC variants are on record (11 BugsInPy, 7 QuixBugs canonical, 3 declared) plus 4 D4 sensitivity variants and 2
   non-comparable legacy rows. Of the BugsInPy variants 9/11 are < 0.5; all 7 canonical QuixBugs variants are < 0.5 (§8). The only BugsInPy variants ≥ 0.5 are two
   post-hoc subsets (0.5104 "units not importing `re` ex-SP-2"; 0.5115 re.purge-rescore ex-SP-2, computed by D4).

(BugsInPy, the more conservative unit, only 10–12 clusters so bootstrap intervals are likely somewhat too narrow), *unit family* (same function across bugs).
`perm p (AUROC<0.5)` is the probability under the within-cluster permutation null of an AUROC at least as low as observed.

## 4. Task 3 — Zero-score decomposition and tie handling

`s = 0` means the SBG V5 distance is exactly 0 (variant is indistinguishable from base under the protocol inputs; for unevaluated pairs under the ALL denominator the score is imputed 0).
The exact decomposition AUROC = P(s>0|pos)·P(s=0|neg) + ½·P(s=0|pos)·P(s=0|neg) + P(s>0|pos)·P(s>0|neg)·AUROC(s>0 subset) is verified numerically for every set in `03_zero_decomposition.json`.

{{T:T5_zero}}

* **Zero fractions** (positives vs negatives): BP-v7-EVAL 51 % (21/41) vs 29 % (31/106); BP-v8-exSP2 63 % (26/41) vs 52 % (34/66); QC-v7-ALL 80 % (32/40) vs 49 % (55/112); QC-v8-exSP2 82 % (32/39) vs 65 % (47/72);
  QD-v7-EVAL 33 % (8/24) vs 61 % (46/75).
* **The hurdle difference P(s>0|pos) − P(s>0|neg) is negative in every canonical/BugsInPy set** (Fisher two-sided p = 0.020 for BP-v7-EVAL and 0.0007 for QC-v7-ALL, PRE-REG sets;
  not significant on the post-hoc ex-SP-2 sets: 0.24 and 0.08). In words: negatives (SP transforms) are *more* often detected as "changed" than real fixes. That is what pushes AUROC below 0.5; it is
  not mere noise. In the declared regime the sign flips (+0.28 for QD-v7-EVAL, Fisher p = 0.020).
* **The repository's claim** ("63–82 % of positives score 0 … AUROC cannot exceed chance in expectation", `REAL_BUG_GENERALISATION_AUDIT.md`) **is not generally valid.** With ties counted ½, AUROC depends on
  the *relative* zero rates and on the ordering of the non-zero scores. E.g. BP-v8-exSP2: 63 % of positives and 52 % of negatives are zero; the tie-aware AUROC is 0.497, but the same data give
  0.334 if every tie is resolved against the positive and 0.660 if every tie is resolved in favour of the positive. A high zero rate makes the AUROC *imprecise and dominated by ties*; it does not by itself
  force it to 0.5 or below, and the post-hoc ex-SP-2 values (0.497, 0.444) are not "the correct given the zero-score rate" values the audit claims — they are consistent with a range of true values.
* **AUROC among s > 0 only** (0.55–0.95 across sets; e.g. 0.82 for BP-v8-exSP2, 0.95 for QC-v8-exSP2) is **a post-hoc selection on the score** and is biased (it conditions on the outcome of the very statistic being evaluated; ranking
  within a censored subset says nothing about the full population). It is shown only to illustrate that the ordering *among detected pairs* is not the problem; it must not be quoted as performance.
* Tie mass (probability that a random pos–neg pair is tied): 0.15 (BP-v7-EVAL) to 0.55 (QC-v8-exSP2). In the ex-SP-2 sets 33 % (BugsInPy) and 54 % (QuixBugs) of all pos–neg pairs are ties.
  Tie-handling alone moves QC-v8-exSP2 from 0.177 to 0.712. A real-valued thresholded detector (`auroc_binary_detector`: s>0 as the score) gives 0.44 / 0.42 on the ex-SP-2 sets.

(`static_ast`, `static_token`, `exception_fraction`, `call_count`, `sbg_v3`); the pre-registered sixth comparator ("dev-selected model") is not present in the external pair files, so the family is of size 5.
Columns `F:*` (not shown here; see `04_baselines_table.csv`) are D4's trivial features computed from the vendored files.

{{T:T6_baselines}}

Key observations:

* **BP-v7-EVAL (PRE-REG):** `static_ast` 0.636 [0.561, 0.710] is higher than sbg_v5 0.410 by +0.226 (Holm-adjusted p = 0.002). `static_token`, `exception_fraction`, `call_count` and `sbg_v3` do not differ
  significantly from sbg_v5 after Holm. In other words SBG V5 is not significantly better than any trivial baseline on BugsInPy, and `static_ast` is significantly better.
* **QC-v7-ALL (PRE-REG):** `static_token` is 0.2095 (far *below* chance, i.e. static distance ranks real bugs lower than SP negatives); `call_count` and `exception_fraction` beat sbg_v5 after Holm.
* **QD-v7-EVAL (declared regime, PRE-REG):** sbg_v5 0.702 is significantly *above* `static_ast`, `static_token`, `exception_fraction` and `sbg_v3` (Holm-adjusted p = 0.002, 0.002, 0.042, 0.031) and not distinguishable from `call_count` (0.645, p = 0.27). It is not above `sbg_state` (0.738, not in the Holm family, Δ = −0.036 for sbg_v5, p = 0.15). The static baselines score *below* 0.5 here (e.g. `static_token` 0.182), which is the same shortcut direction discussed below — so "beating" them does not show behavioural sensitivity.
* `sbg_state` (not a member of the Holm family; exploratory) is higher than sbg_v5 in every set shown (BP-v7-EVAL 0.511, Δ = +0.102, raw p = 0.0004; QC-v7-ALL 0.582).

**Label–source shortcut check (POST-HOC EXPLORATORY).** For each pair D4 computes features that need no execution at all: number of changed lines and tokens between base and variant files
(from `SBG/external/{bugsinpy,quixbugs}` vendored files), relative/absolute edit sizes, whether the variant text equals `ast.unparse(ast.parse(variant))` (the form every SP transformer emits), and whether comments are lost.
`F:*` AUROCs < 0.5 mean the feature ranks negatives *higher* than positives; the reversed value is 1 − AUROC and is only an **upper-bound shortcut diagnostic**, not a predictor (the direction is chosen after seeing labels).

{{T:T7_shortcut}}

* `variant_is_unparse_canonical`: AUROC 0.000 on every set → it is 1 for every negative and 0 for every positive in BugsInPy and in both QuixBugs corpora: a perfect, behaviour-free label predictor (SP transformers emit `ast.unparse` output;
  developer files are not canonical). `comment_loss` gives the same perfect separation on BugsInPy.
* `edit_lines` AUROC 0.084 on BP-v7-EVAL (0.916 reversed): real fixes are very small edits, SP negatives are larger rewrites. `edit_tokens` reversed: 0.675 on QC-v7-ALL, 0.884 on QC-v8-exSP2.
* Reversed `static_token` on QD-v7-EVAL = 1 − 0.182 = 0.818 and reversed `edit_tokens` = 1 − 0.334 = 0.666, against SBG V5's 0.702 on the same 99 pairs. A trivial static feature (direction chosen post hoc) therefore reaches or exceeds SBG V5 in the one regime
  where SBG V5 is above chance, so even that result cannot be attributed to behavioural sensitivity.
* **Consequence.** The external negative class ("fixed vs SBG-transformed fixed") is not drawn from the same population as the positive class ("buggy vs fixed"). Any external AUROC — high or low — mixes (i) whether SBG measures behaviour change with (ii) how SBG's scoring responds to
  *source-style* differences (comment loss, canonical unparse, edit size). Because the SP-2/SP-3 negatives are where sbg_v5 scores worst (SP-2-only AUROC 0.31 BugsInPy, 0.27 QuixBugs; SP-3 0.39 / 0.30 — §7, v7 committed scores), the sub-0.5 result is better read as
  "SBG is more perturbed by SP-2/SP-3 transformed code than by the real fix" — a statement about the *negatives*, which are partly invalid (7 statically invalid + unknown number not audited).
  This is a design confound that no amount of resampling can repair.

## 6. Task 5 — Power, minimum detectable effect, and what each interval rules out

**A. What the observed intervals rule out (computed from the observed bootstrap, not simulation; `05_power.json → what_cis_rule_out`).**

{{T:T8_rule_out}}

* BugsInPy v8 ex-SP-2 (0.497): the bug-cluster 95 % CI [0.401, 0.593] and the project-cluster CI [0.425, 0.597] exclude a true AUROC ≥ 0.6 only narrowly (the unit-family CI [0.413, 0.600] does not exclude it); the one-sided 95 % upper bounds are 0.577 / 0.579. With 11 project clusters the bootstrap is optimistic (see simulated half-widths below: ≈ ±0.11–0.12), so
  "≥ 0.6 ruled out" is *borderline* at project level; it is not borderline for ≥ 0.75 or ≥ 0.897.
* QuixBugs v8 ex-SP-2 (0.444): CI [0.356, 0.536] rules out ≥ 0.6.
* **No interval of an ex-SP-2 set excludes 0.5**, so neither "better than chance" nor "worse than chance" is established there; likewise "equivalent to chance within ±0.05" is not established (the lower bounds are 0.40–0.43 for BugsInPy and 0.36 for QuixBugs). Only the pre-registered v7 sets have CIs/p-values excluding 0.5 (below it).
* QuixBugs declared EVALUABLE (0.702 [0.580, 0.825]) excludes ≤ 0.5 and ≥ 0.897 but includes 0.75; it does not rule out V10-level performance of ≈ 0.8 in that regime.

**B. Simulation-based power / MDE (POST-HOC APPROXIMATION; assumptions below).** Model: a censored binormal. Latent z = μ·y + √ρ_p·u_project + √ρ_b·v_bug + √(1−ρ_p−ρ_b)·ε, score = max(z − κ, 0), with κ chosen so that P(s=0|neg) equals the observed negative
zero fraction of the design; the observed cluster structure and class counts are used; the true AUROC θ(μ) is obtained by numerical integration. Test: cluster-jackknife Wald test of θ̂ > 0.5 (one-sided 2.5 %; t-quantile with G−1 df for project clusters), 1 500 simulated data sets per cell.
Intra-cluster correlation ρ is estimated with a rank-based one-way ANOVA ICC **on negatives only** (no use of positive labels) and two scenarios are added (ρ = 0 and ρ_p = ρ_b = 0.2). This is a *simplified generative model*: real SBG scores are not binormal, the clustering of positives is not modelled beyond the ICC proxy, and the jackknife SE is an approximation. Use as order-of-magnitude only.

{{T:T9c_icc}}

{{T:T9_power}}

Power curves in the `rho_est` scenario:

{{T:T9b_powercurve}}

* **MDE at 80 % power ≈ 0.61–0.68 true AUROC** (BugsInPy bug-cluster ≈ 0.61–0.66, project-cluster ≈ 0.64–0.68; QuixBugs ≈ 0.64–0.65); mean 95 % CI half-width ≈ 0.08–0.12 (BugsInPy) and 0.09–0.10 (QuixBugs). For a true AUROC of 0.54–0.58 the simulated power is only about 0.1–0.5 (see the curves).
* The simulated size at μ = 0 is 2.3–3.9 % (nominal 2.5 % one-sided) — the jackknife test is roughly calibrated under the model.
* Consequence for the claim "SBG does not generalise": a CI half-width of 0.1 means the external data can say "not ≥ 0.6" but cannot say "= 0.5" or "< 0.55". The *decisive* evidence against a large effect (AUROC ≥ 0.7, let alone 0.897) is solid; the evidence against a *moderate* effect (0.55–0.6) is weak. Conversely, the pre-registered below-chance values are
  statistically significant yet driven by a confounded negative class (§5).

## 10. Limitations of this assessment

* D4 does not re-run SBG; it analyses stored per-pair scores. The committed per-pair scores are taken as given (the D2 reproduction subagent is responsible for re-execution).
* Cluster bootstrap with 10–12 projects is unreliable (intervals tend to be too narrow); permutation tests are within-cluster only and treat bugs as exchangeable within a cluster.
* The power model is a simplified censored-binormal; MDE values are order-of-magnitude, not guarantees.
* Edit-size features use the vendored files in `SBG/external/*` and the pair-file paths; they were designed *after* seeing that labels might be source-confounded. They quantify the confound, they are not alternative detectors.
* The Holm family has 5 members (pre-registered family of 6 minus the dev-selected model, unavailable externally).
* Label reliability of positives (are the BugsInPy fixes behaviour-changing for the sampled function?) and of negatives (are SP-2/SP-3 variants truly equivalent?) is not assessed here beyond the 7 audited invalid SP-2 negatives; remaining invalid negatives would further lower the AUROC.
